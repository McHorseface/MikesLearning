### History — Ireland → England → America
| Resource | What it covers |
|---|---|
| *How the Irish Saved Civilization* — Thomas Cahill | Early medieval Ireland: monasteries, culture, Ireland's outsized role before colonization |
| *The Course of Irish History* — Moody & Martin (eds.) | Single-volume scholarly history from prehistory through the Troubles — the standard starting point |
| *Ireland and the British Empire* — Kevin Kenny | The colonial relationship: how England colonized, exploited, and shaped Ireland |
| *The Oxford History of the British Empire, Vol. I* — Nicholas Canny | Origins of empire — Ireland's central role in early English colonization, connecting to the American colonies |
| *Emigrants and Exiles* — Kerby Miller | The definitive account of Irish emigration to America and the formation of Irish-American identity |
| [CELT — Corpus of Electronic Texts](https://celt.ucc.ie) | University College Cork's free archive of Irish historical texts, annals, and primary sources in English and Irish |
| [Ireland Story Bookshop](https://www.wesleyjohnston.com/users/ireland/bookshop/pre_1600.htm) | Annotated reading lists organized by era — Stone Age through 1600 |

> **On the history thread:** Read this as *one story*, not three. Cahill (early Ireland) → Kenny (colonization) → Kerby Miller (Atlantic crossing) reads almost like a trilogy. The Oxford History fills in the English imperial context. CELT is where you go when you want primary sources — actual annals, chronicles, and texts from medieval Ireland.

## A Note on the Math → Data Science connection

Linear algebra is the mathematical backbone of almost everything in data science and ML — matrix operations, transformations, eigenvalues, and gradient descent all live there. The bucket-list math goal and the data science goal are the same path. Calc II is the grind. Linear Algebra is the payoff. Desmos and Wolfram Alpha are your best friends the whole way through.
