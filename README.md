### History — Ireland → England → America
| Resource | What it covers |
|---|---|
| *How the Irish Saved Civilization* — Thomas Cahill | Early medieval Ireland: monasteries, culture, and Ireland's outsized role before the colonial era |
| *The Course of Irish History* — Moody & Martin (eds.) | Single-volume scholarly history from prehistory through the Troubles — the standard starting point |
| *Ireland and the British Empire* — Kevin Kenny | The colonial relationship directly: how England colonized, exploited, and shaped Ireland |
| *The Oxford History of the British Empire, Vol. I* — Nicholas Canny | Origins of empire — Ireland's central role in early English colonization and how it prefigured America |
| *Emigrants and Exiles* — Kerby Miller | The definitive account of Irish emigration to America and the formation of Irish-American identity |
| [Ireland Story Bookshop](https://www.wesleyjohnston.com/users/ireland/bookshop/pre_1600.htm) | Annotated reading lists organized by era — Stone Age through 1600, free online |

> **On the history thread:** This is best read as *one story*, not three. Cahill (early Ireland) → Kenny (colonization) → Kerby Miller (Atlantic crossing) reads almost like a trilogy. The Oxford History fills in the English imperial context that connects Ireland to the American colonies.

## A Note on the Math → Data Science connection

Linear algebra is the mathematical backbone of almost everything in data science and ML — matrix operations, transformations, eigenvalues, and gradient descent all live there. If the bucket-list math goal and the data science goal feel like two separate things, they're not. Getting through Linear Algebra will make the data science click in a way that no tutorial can shortcut. Calc II is the grind. Linear Algebra is the payoff.
